### 1.3 Clauses (`PARAPHRASE`, researcher-transcribed)

**3/1: what a share represents.**
- A share represents undivided ownership in the company's capital or assets. These may include tangible assets, usufructs and debts.
- `REPORTED OBSERVATION`: an explanatory note says that where tangible assets exist alongside cash or debts, tangible assets must be **at least one-third**. The note reportedly says it was added in preparation for a later edition.
- See the conflict in 1.4.

**3/2: buying and selling.**
- Shares in companies with permissible activities may be bought and sold for cash or for permissible deferred payment.
- Investment, and trading to benefit from price changes, are permitted under the stated conditions.

**3/3: participating in order to change the activity.**
- Participation intended to change a company's activity to Shari'ah-compliant is described as applying to someone capable of making that change. An example is a shareholder able to influence the first general meeting.

**3/4: company activity and financial screening.**
- The principal activity must be permissible. The researcher reports exceptions and criteria for companies with interest-bearing deposits or loans:

| Sub-clause | Researcher's paraphrase |
|---|---|
| 3/4/1 | The company's constitutional documents must not list interest or prohibited goods or services as objectives. |
| 3/4/2 | Total interest-bearing debt, short- and long-term, must not exceed **30% of market capitalization**. The standard reportedly still describes interest-bearing debt as prohibited in principle. |
| 3/4/3 | Interest-bearing deposits, across short, medium and long terms, must not exceed **30%**. `REPORTED OBSERVATION`: the Arabic refers to the market capitalization of the company's shares. The English phrase "market capitalization of total equity" may be ambiguous. |
| 3/4/4 | Income from prohibited sources must not exceed **5% of the company's total revenue**. `REPORTED OBSERVATION`: the Arabic term is reported as `إجمالي إيرادات الشركة`, meaning total or gross revenue, not net profit. This term is the researcher's report, not a verified quotation of the clause. The reported scope covers prohibited activities, ownership of prohibited assets, and other routes. If a source of income is not disclosed, further effort should be made to identify it. |
| 3/4/5 | Ratios are calculated from the latest budget or approved financial statements. |
| 3/4/6 | **Purification.** See 1.5. |
| 3/4/7 | The conditions reportedly apply whether the institution trades for itself or for others, through a fund, as an agent, and so on. |
| 3/4/8 | The conditions must hold throughout the holding period. If they are violated, the investor must exit. **No grace period was identified in the researcher's notes.** |

**3/5: margin.** Margin trading funded by an interest-bearing loan is prohibited, and so is pledging shares for such a loan.

**3/6: short selling.** Short selling is prohibited. A broker's promise to lend shares for delivery does not change the reported ruling.

**3/7: resale before settlement.** After a sale is concluded and liability (daman) has passed to the buyer, resale is reportedly permissible even if final settlement has not completed.

**3/8: regulation.** State regulators may organize share trading through licensed brokers.

**3/9: lending shares.** Shares may not be lent as a qard.

**3/10: pledging shares.** Permissible shares may be pledged as collateral.

**3/11 – 3/14: forward and derivative contracts.**
- Salam, futures, options and swaps in shares are reported as prohibited.
- The notes do not assign each contract type to a specific clause within 3/11–3/14. See also the SS 20 cross-reference in 2.3.

**3/15: leasing shares.** Leasing shares is reported as prohibited. That includes arrangements like securities lending, where shares are sold and equivalent shares are returned later.

**3/16: i'arah (a loan for use without compensation).**
- Reported as permitted for collateral or for dividends.
- The recipient may sell only to enforce the collateral.

**3/17: assets entirely in cash.** If a company's assets are only cash, for example during subscription, before operations or in liquidation, its shares are reportedly sold only at nominal value and subject to the handover condition.

**3/18: assets entirely in debts.** If a company's assets are entirely debts, the rules on trading debts apply.

**3/19: mixed assets.**
- The reported rule depends on the company's principal activity:
  - If it trades in goods, usufructs or rights, its shares may reportedly be sold without applying the rules on currency exchange (sarf) or debt, **if the market value of its tangible assets, usufructs and rights is at least 30% of total assets**.
  - If its activity is gold, silver or currency, the sarf rules apply.
- **Threshold conflict:** `UNRESOLVED`. See 1.4.
- **Financing-company qualification (reported gist only):** the clause contains a qualification about companies whose principal activity is debt or financing facilities. For such companies, share trading must follow the debt-trading rules.
  - `ARABIC TEXT NOT PROVIDED — DO NOT QUOTE`.
  - This is not a verified quotation.

**3/20: disguised debt trading.** A company must not add a small amount of tangible assets merely to disguise a transaction that is in substance debt trading.

**Sections 4–5: bonds.**
- Interest-bearing bonds, including zero-coupon and prize bonds, are reported as prohibited to issue, trade, pledge or endorse.
- The reported Shari'ah alternative is investment sukuk under SS 17.

**Appendix B: rationale for the 3/4 exception.**
- The reported grounds are:
  - removal of hardship (`raf' al-haraj`);
  - widespread prevalence (`umum al-balwa`);
  - the majority-part rule;
  - permissibility of dealing with something whose majority is lawful;
  - some jurists' `tafriq al-safqa`.
- Some rulings were reportedly adopted by majority, not unanimously.
- Appendix B reportedly also states the one-third tangible-assets threshold (see 1.4).

### 1.4 Unresolved conflict: tangible-assets threshold (`UNRESOLVED`)

| Location | Reported threshold | PDF / printed page |
|---|---|---|
| Clause 3/19, clause text | 30% (of total assets) | 13 / 573 |
| Clause 3/1, explanatory note | one-third | 7 / 567 |
| Appendix B | one-third | 24 / 584 |

- This record does not decide which threshold governs.
- Open decision 17 (section 3) asks whether the conflict can be settled from an authoritative AAOIFI source or needs a Shari'ah adviser.

### 1.5 Purification (clause 3/4/6), as reported

- **Who:** an investor or trader holding shares at the **end of the fiscal period**.
- **Not required** if the shares are sold before period end. That holds whether the investment made a profit or a loss, and whether or not dividends were paid.
- **Intermediaries:** an intermediary, agent or manager does not purify its own commission.
- **Formula, recorded exactly as reported:**

  ```
  (total prohibited income / total shares) × shares held
  ```

  - This is **not** a dividend-times-ratio formula, and it must not be replaced by one unless that interpretation is explicitly approved.
- **Use of the amount:** it must not be used for any purpose, including paying taxes.
- **Institutions** reportedly purify their own and managed holdings.
- **Intermediaries** must explain the mechanism to clients and may offer it as a paid or free service.

### 2.1 SS 12: Sharikah (Musharakah) and Modern Corporations

| Clause | Researcher's paraphrase |
|---|---|
| 4/1/2/6 | No purchase with an interest-bearing loan from a broker or anyone else, and no pledging of shares for such a loan (margin). |

| 4/1/2/8 | Trading through licensed brokers. |
| 4/1/2/11 | Pledging shares is reportedly permitted, subject to the company's rules. |

- **Flag (`UNRESOLVED`):** preferred shares with priority on dividends must be flagged separately for review by a Shari'ah adviser.
